# Fits of Rage: Effects on the Brain and Others

## 🧠 Neurobiological Impact (Neurotransmitters)

When a person experiences a **fit of rage**, several key **neurotransmitters and brain structures** are involved:

### 1. **Amygdala Overactivation**
- The amygdala, responsible for emotional processing (especially fear and aggression), becomes hyperactive.
- This can override logical thinking and impulse control.

### 2. **Decreased Prefrontal Cortex Activity**
- The **prefrontal cortex**, which governs reasoning, judgment, and self-control, is **suppressed** during intense anger.
- Result: impulsive, irrational actions.

### 3. **Neurotransmitter Changes**
| Neurotransmitter | Effect in Rage Fits |
|------------------|---------------------|
| **Dopamine**     | May rise temporarily, creating a sense of power or satisfaction during outbursts. |
| **Norepinephrine (Noradrenaline)** | Spikes during rage, increasing heart rate, blood pressure, and alertness — fueling the "fight" response. |
| **Serotonin**    | Often **low** in individuals prone to rage; this can reduce mood regulation and increase impulsivity. |
| **Cortisol**     | Stress hormone that increases, particularly in repeated or chronic anger episodes, impairing memory and immune function. |

---

## 👥 Impact on Others

### 1. **Emotional Harm**
- Creates fear, insecurity, or emotional trauma in others, especially children or those in close relationships.
- Repeated exposure can lead to **anxiety**, **depression**, or **PTSD** in victims.

### 2. **Relational Damage**
- Breaks trust and creates distance in relationships.
- Leads to avoidance, silence, or reciprocal aggression.
