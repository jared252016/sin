# Dissensions: Neurobiological and Relational Impact

## 🧠 Neurological Effects of Dissensions

Dissensions—persistent disagreements, conflict, or division—can activate and reshape the brain’s stress and emotional systems, especially when sustained over time.

### 1. **Neurotransmitters Involved**
- **Cortisol (Stress Hormone)**: Elevated during ongoing conflict, leading to:
  - Impaired memory (hippocampus)
  - Heightened anxiety (amygdala)
  - Reduced impulse control (prefrontal cortex)

- **Dopamine**: May become dysregulated. In hostile group dynamics or antagonism:
  - Can reinforce aggressive behaviors
  - May reduce reward from cooperation or empathy

- **Serotonin**: Decreased levels are linked with:
  - Increased irritability
  - Aggressive or divisive tendencies
  - Lower mood and emotional regulation

- **Oxytocin**: Typically promotes bonding and trust, but:
  - In group-based dissensions, can *increase* in-group bias
  - Leads to stronger “us vs. them” division

## 🤝 Effects of Dissensions on Others

### 1. **Emotional Climate**
- Heightened tension, mistrust, and fear
- People withdraw or become defensive
- Creates emotional exhaustion and burnout

### 2. **Group Dynamics**
- Breakdown of unity (e.g., families, churches, teams)
- Factions form, deepening division
- Collaboration, empathy, and love diminish

### 3. **Spiritual Impact**
- Grieves the Spirit (cf. Ephesians 4:30-31)
- Hinders worship and fellowship (cf. Matthew 5:23-24)
- Opposes the fruit of the Spirit (cf. Galatians 5:22)

> “Let us therefore make every effort to do what leads to peace and to mutual edification.”  
> — Romans 14:19 NIV

---

## 🛑 Summary

Dissensions trigger stress systems in the brain, impair healthy communication, and spread emotional toxicity. They divide communities and hinder godly unity. God calls us to the ministry of reconciliation (2 Corinthians 5:18) and peace.
